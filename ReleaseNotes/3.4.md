Tensor Tycoon becomes a whole industry. Start with a founding kit, train your models through a real launch sequence, and choose how each one goes out into the world. Build a group of companies around your lab: design chips and build fabs, run science labs whose discoveries change the rest of the game, win government programmes, buy and clean training data, grow agent swarms into AI organisations, and finance it all like a public company. Base yourself in the US, Romania, Italy, France or Denmark, watch seven regions set national AI strategies, and race rivals that specialise and answer your launches. The alien chip no longer gets stuck.

### A better start
- **Founding kit**: pick starting cards on the new-game screen: 39 of them, from a genius research team and RL pioneers to an angel GPU cluster, a rich uncle, an alien signal or a time traveller's notes, with a few handicaps (a glass cannon, an impatient board) that buy you points. Play to a points budget, take anything in sandbox mode, or start in genius mode, where research also runs four times faster.
- **Reinforcement-learning lab**: level it up for faster reasoning and agent gains.
- **Starter quests**: an opening checklist with small rewards that teaches the game as you play.
- **Company stages**: grow from Garage to Startup, Scale-up, Frontier Lab, Hyperscaler and Civilisational on clear criteria (rank, revenue share, compute share, people). Each stage unlocks a power: enterprise-first launches, stronger lobbying, government-first launches and more sovereign offers, cheaper fabs, early fusion. The Progression page shows where you stand and what's next.
- **Stages open real decisions**: term loans and two subsidiaries at Scale-up; security clearances and classified work, regional entities and chip design teams at Frontier Lab; fabs of your own, a third science lab and AI organisations beyond 1,000 agents at Hyperscaler; the last moonshots at Civilisational. Each locked action says which stage opens it, and saves keep anything they already built.

### Headquarters
- **Five countries, 12 cities**: the US, Romania, Italy, France and Denmark, each with real tax, payroll, salary, office and grant differences, its own talent pool and local partners, and a premium selection screen with licensed photos.
- Grants, partner offers and contracts from home arrive in your inbox.
- **Real R&D relief**: Romania's relief counts only against profit and carries forward (and its 1% minimum turnover tax applies from 2024), Italy's offsets payroll taxes up to €5M a year, Denmark refunds the tax value of R&D losses up to its cap, and France's research credit pays 30% up to €100M of R&D a year and 5% above. Losses carry forward against future tax, and the Finance page's Tax card explains your country's rules.
- **Talent and offices abroad**: a startup hires locally at first; once it's known, people relocate from abroad at international pay, so there's no permanent cap. Satellite offices (Team page) hire from their own city at local pay, on-costs and rent, 100 desks at a time.
- **Building at home**: build incentives come off the price of each phase you build in your home country and show in the quote. Danish wind, Romanian hydro, Italian solar and French nuclear contracts are offered at the European sites, and each site has its own grid queue.
- **National programmes**: grants arrive only once the real programme existed; national contracts are sized to the state's AI budget, hosted in the country and need a minimum safety score. Co-fund a national compute centre (priority GPU hours) or endow a university chair.
- **Rules where they apply**: US federal rules reach US labs and US soil only; the EU AI Act applies to any model sold in the EU. EU countries run their own policy dials that drift each January with reasons, real dated events such as the 2025 AI Diffusion Rule tiers play out, and regions go through recessions.
- **Selection screen**: one comparison row per city, with badges that tell real rules from game estimates; the garage picker lists sites only for a US headquarters.

### Training you can watch
- **Model Lab**: choose how many experts a mixture-of-experts model has (routing costs throughput until you research the fixes), how memory is handled (checkpointing or offloading when the model doesn't fit), hot spares that keep a big cluster running, quantisation-aware training for cheaper low-precision serving, and a dangerous-capability evaluation stage. Every choice shows what it costs and buys.
- **Pre-training review**: before a run starts you get a review of the design, data, compute, memory and risks, with an honest forecast range (hidden until you research scaling laws).
- **Launch sequence and command centre**: a frontier run starts site by site, cluster activation and power draw rising, then lives in a command centre with its loss and held-out validation curves, a cluster map built from the run's own telemetry (click a cell to see its faults), useful throughput against plan, gradient norms, milestones and events. Animations can be reduced.
- **Act on a run**: run a diagnostic sweep, investigate a loss spike, red-team it mid-run, end pre-training early with the projected result and range, or extend it with more tokens, each with its price and estimate. A cancelled run can keep its last checkpoint.
- **Runs keep their data**: a run locks in its data plan when it starts; data bought, processed or lost later doesn't change it.
- **Results**: a run report with what each fault, bug and corrupt node cost, the learning-rate credit, a real scaling ladder, and "put it to work" shortcuts. Landmark firsts (a trillion parameters, 500,000 chips, past the frontier, the first independently verified discovery…) get a ceremony; cluster firsts count only once a quarter of the run is done.
- **Evaluations**: commission dangerous-capability evaluations for a forked checkpoint or an older model (three weeks, about 1% of what it cost to train), or launch it with a full system card. Under the EU AI Act, near-frontier models without them are fined.
- **Model families**: put Core, Spark and Mote tiers under your flagship. Distil a smaller tier from any model you own, choosing its size and data budget; see exactly how much it keeps in each domain and how cheaply it serves. Post-train with reasoning RL, agentic training, safety tuning, long context or a code, science or medicine specialisation; each pass ships as a point release, and a merge ships v1.5 with half the trade-offs undone. Route free chat users and enterprise contracts to cheaper tiers, and see how much each tier cannibalises its siblings.
- **Specialists and foundations**: fork a coding, science, medicine, reasoning, vision & voice or robotics specialist into a family without touching the flagship; Model Routing sells the whole family through one endpoint. A new flagship runs the family's recipe at half cost. Models keep the know-how they were trained with, so a later pass only teaches its own field and a student never overtakes its teacher.

### Launches
- **Choose how each model launches**: blog post, stealth drop, staged rollout, keynote, open weights, enterprise-first, government-first or benchmark-first, with a price stance (premium, match, undercut) and a safety posture (short card, standard, full system card with red-team report). Every option shows what it will do.
- **The world answers**: day-one rushes that fade, press quotes from the people of AI, moves in standing with the public, developers, researchers and governments, and rivals who answer weeks later.
- **Two more ways to launch**: a research preview (quarter price, rate-limited, feedback that makes the next pass cheaper) and limited-access testing (trusted testers first, safer models, sales later). Pricing stances now hold for their 120 days, stealth drops rarely provoke rivals, and family tiers don't use up your next launch's attention.
- **The market keeps moving**: a new #1 wins enterprise leads from weaker rivals, contracts can leave at renewal for a clearly better rival, a developer ecosystem builds up, and investors react to rank changes.
- **Reasoning effort**: let reasoning models think less or more: more thinking scores higher and costs more to serve.
- **Launch-day report**: a week after release, sign-ups, traffic, quotes, benchmark reception, rival reactions and a verdict against your plan, plus a history of every launch.

### Chips and fabs
- **The alien chip no longer gets stuck**: exclusive partner deals can't lock out your own silicon, runs on alien chips shrink instead of stalling when chips fail, alien chips run on air, and the Alien Tech page can lease a cage for them in one click.
- **Alien progress timeline**: every stage from first search to v3 silicon, with ETAs, what's blocking it and the button that moves it on. Your own alien fab line (the pilot line is free), v2 and v3 generations, yield discoveries at volume, and errata that put chips you don't understand at risk.
- **Semiconductors**: hire chip design teams and design accelerators for process nodes from 16 nm to A10-class, with time, cost and respin odds shown before you commit. Book wafers at four fictional foundries or build your own fab, with power, water, incentives and a yield ramp. Advanced packaging and HBM are separate bottlenecks. Yields come from defect density and improve as you learn; every die is binned into Ultra, Standard and Lite. Earthquakes, shortages and export rules can cut supply. Sell to clouds, sovereign funds and, if you choose, rival labs (they get stronger), or keep the chips for yourself.
- **Chips from the silicon up**: performance comes from the node, die area and architecture fixed at tape-out. Choose a training or inference layout and an interconnect (switched, Ethernet or co-packaged optics), each with its own domain size, cost, yield and respin risk. Your chips' software matures with a compiler team and use; a new instruction set starts behind. A compute-economics panel compares your cost per PFLOP-day with the best merchant chip.
- **Honest chip sales**: only chips you sell leave your stock, rival labs pay from their own cash, defunct and export-blocked buyers stop ordering, exclusive-supply promises are kept, deposits come back, and retired chips still fill their orders. Ports reuse a proven design one node away. Partner custom-silicon programmes take no new chips; existing ones keep running.
- A science lab that reproduces the 2D transistor opens a 2D-channel process node years before the industry.

### Training data
- **Data is a real resource**: 17 data types, each dataset rated on 11 quality dimensions, in a new Data hub with an inventory and quality radar.
- **Quality beats size**: a small, relevant, clean dataset can beat a huge noisy one. The Model Lab's data plan shows the tokens your design gets in each domain and warns, for example, when a coding model is short on code.
- **Buy, license, go exclusive or partner**; hire annotators for preference data and expert demonstrations; clean data with deduplication, filtering, verification and decontamination; have your own models write synthetic data (too much of it causes model collapse).
- Rare archives come up as private sales and sealed-bid auctions against rival labs, and poor-provenance data can draw lawsuits.
- **Data has limits**: synthetic data can't teach more than the model that wrote it or outweigh your real data, and repeats itself as you make more; RLHF needs preference labels from the last year; datasets under research-only licences block open-weights releases. Lapsed licences can be renewed for 90 days with your processed copy, and auction bids count transfer costs.

### Science labs
- **Eight kinds of laboratory in 13 cities**: materials, robotics, scientific computing, electronics, self-driving labs, biology, energy and semiconductors.
- **Real research**: 41 projects in 12 fields go through ten stages from hypothesis to commercialisation. Each stage shows its odds and why; experiments get rerun, and promising ideas die at reproduction or in manufacturing.
- **Discovery reports**: what it means, what it's worth, what it does to your other projects and how rivals will respond. Then patent it, keep it secret, publish it, spin it out or feed it to a moonshot.
- **Discoveries change the game**: lower power bills, cheaper batteries, earlier fusion, better custom chips, cheaper inference, sharper maths and robotics in your models, product lines that open sooner, better moonshot odds.
- **New technology**: some discoveries open things outright: fusion plants, early quantum chemistry, lossless power delivery for new campuses, a 2D-channel chip process.
- **Publishing spreads**: rival science arms reproduce what you publish within months and their models gain the know-how. Patents pay per licensee, spin-outs pay milestones, stopping a project is never better than a dead end, and every reproduced discovery's lab notebooks become training data. Unique AI breakthroughs get reports with publish, keep or license choices.
- **Company history**: one filterable timeline of your stages, rounds, launches, firsts, discoveries and programmes.

### Government work
- **Advanced Projects**: 14 agencies post AI work. Bid against rival labs with explained win odds and a full cost breakdown, under fixed-price, cost-plus, grant or prize terms, with milestone payments, audits, cure notices and budget cuts.
- **Programmes** go from feasibility through research, experiments, prototype, independent testing and validation; failed trials mean rework.
- **Classified work**: build secure labs and cleared datacenter halls, clear your staff (it takes months, and some can't be cleared), and decide what to do with a classified discovery.
- **Honest programmes**: staff and swarms on a programme work only for it; a mobilisation planner shows every step (clearances, desks, compute, bays) with months and cost and blocks bids you can't start in time; facilities draw real power from their site and need cleared security officers; Secret work stays at home on sites you run; failed trials cost rework.
- **A real customer**: agencies spend from yearly budgets that every award draws down (yours and rivals'), shrink in recessions and cancel work when over-committed. Rivals carry out the contracts they win and can default. What you do with classified discoveries changes how far agencies trust you; clearances cost a pay premium, attract poachers and are reinvestigated every five years; Secret work stops your labs publishing in its field.

### AI organisations
- **Swarms grow into organisations**: specialist and supervisor teams, each running its own model, with compute and money budgets, verification, oversight, monthly reports and a project history. Fourteen kinds, including software engineering, scientific discovery, infrastructure, robotics, finance and experiment coordination.
- **Output you can explain**: it depends on your models' skills and domains, with diminishing returns and coordination overhead that supervisors win back. A "why this output" panel shows the sum.
- **Real effects**: cheaper and better products, likelier moonshots, fewer outages, a smaller power bill, faster experiments, faster science labs and new training techniques. Existing swarms keep working and can become an organisation in one click.
- **Scaling with limits**: organisations of a kind pool their effort; very large ones scale less efficiently, need good enough supervisors and more verification as errors compound. Stronger agents unlock new work (automated research, chip yield learning, architecture search, self-optimising serving, automated science, construction planning), and organisations can be given measurable objectives with deadlines. More reliable models make fewer mistakes.

### Corporate finance
- **Subsidiaries**: start or buy companies in robotics, drug discovery, chip design, consumer apps, consulting, cloud or another country. Each has its own CEO, team, strategy and results; sell stakes, list them, spin them off or fold them back in.
- **Borrow like a grown-up company**: covenanted term loans (tested on the last twelve months), convertible notes and green bonds for datacenters, with a credit rating that moves with leverage and interest cover.
- **Payouts**: dividends and buybacks after your IPO, employee tender offers before it.
- **The books**: monthly income statement, balance sheet and cash flow, two-year runway scenarios, a fully diluted cap table and a tax view with transfer pricing.
- **Books that balance**: every dollar is on the income statement, in capex or in a labelled investing or financing line; hardware depreciates over four years and datacenters over twenty; selling assets books a gain or loss. Your valuation counts your cash and subtracts your debt, and a private mark moves at most +60% or −30% a month between rounds. Government programme payments show as their own revenue line.
- **Growth takes a go-to-market**: bookings ramp month by month and your team (salespeople most) limits how many customers you can serve; demand you couldn't book is shown with the reason, and a "why profit moved" card explains each month.
- **Market cycles**: AI-bubble corrections, credit crunches and rate shocks, plus the 2020 and 2022 crashes, change funding, loans, valuations and demand until they pass.
- **More subsidiaries**: an energy developer (cheaper power), security software (attacks succeed less often) and a government research arm (better bids with agencies in its region).

### Nations and rivals
- **National AI strategies**: every region runs a strategy (subsidies, sovereign AI, export controls, data and safety rules, talent visas, energy) that it reviews each January, with the reason for every change. Strategies change your chip, cloud and power costs, the talent you can hire, agency budgets and enterprise demand, each effect shown with its cause.
- **Regional economies**: GDP growth, AI's share of GDP, AI investment and jobs lost to automation.
- **Rivals that specialise**: in coding, science, robotics, consumer, enterprise, sovereign AI or open weights, based on their strengths, their home country's strategy and where you lead. A "Specialisation & why" card on each lab profile explains it.
- **Rivals answer your launches** weeks later with price cuts, competing products or offers to your researchers, and the news says why.
- **Specialists for real**: a rival's focus makes its models stronger there; when you run away with the lead, the strongest unspecialised rival pivots to your weakest field and the news says why. Two new kinds: labs with their own silicon and labs that build efficient models.
- **Intelligence is many skills**: five new domains (hardware engineering, reliability, calibration, architecture design, multilingual), each changing something (chip design, agent errors, safety incidents, research speed, foreign-language demand). API buyers shop by kind of work, so a coding specialist can win coding traffic from a stronger generalist, and enterprise leads are sized by how well your best model does that work.
- **Rival books add up**: parents budget yearly and sometimes cut, R&D buys breakthroughs, buyouts are paid in cash and shares, and compute deals run on chips you actually lease out.

### Balance
- Alien tech still gives a big jump, but once you're more than half a step past everyone else each further step is worth less ("Uncharted regime").
- Subsidiaries are valued on growth and margin together: fast growers that burn cash are worth less, and licence fees are no longer counted twice.
- Chip dies waiting for packaging cost money to hold and lose value as they age; surplus sells to other packagers as known-good die, and the Chief of Staff suggests a fab share with a one-click fix.
- In big AI organisations errors compound, so some verification pays off when your models make many mistakes; "Why this output" shows the best share.
- The Training page's validation curve counts the datasets you bought or licensed, and "First AI to" badges stay on a model after post-training renames it.
- Auto-pricing never lists a model below what serving it costs.
- Data quality now matters beyond size: unchecked, noisy data teaches little, and curated specialist sets are worth their price.
