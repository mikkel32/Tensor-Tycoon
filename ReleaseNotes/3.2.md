The biggest update yet: run millions of AI agents, discover alien technology, sell chips and compute, choose your lab's traits, defend your weights, lobby lawmakers, and play in a world updated through October 2026.

### Agent swarms
- **Swarms**: run anywhere from 10 to 50 million copies of your model on your own inference chips, and give them a mission: research, alien-architecture search, training operations, chip design, alignment, or a digital workforce you rent out.
- **Alignment drift**: big, autonomous, under-watched swarms drift out of alignment. Watch for collusion, sandbagging and escapes onto public clouds.

### Alien technology
- **Discovery**: swarms (or a self-improvement run on Architecture search) can find an architecture no human would design, plus a chip several times faster than the best GPU at a few percent of the cost.
- **Choose what to do with it**: keep it to yourself, sell the chips at huge margins, or license the blueprint to big players. Whatever you sell makes the buyers stronger.

### Selling chips and compute
- **Lease idle GPUs** to the market like a neocloud.
- **Sell your own chips**: run merchant lines for your custom and alien silicon. Set the price, reserve foundry capacity, build a reputation, and decide whether rival labs may buy.

### Money
- **Treasury**: idle cash earns interest, and API prices can track the market automatically.
- **Valuation**: hardware revenue is valued like hardware, and one-off license fees no longer inflate your run-rate.
- **Founder wealth**: sell part of your stake to build a personal net worth.

### Security and policy
- **Weight security**: protect your weights with RAND's SL1–SL5 levels. Attackers come for them in proportion to how close you are to the frontier. Leaks flood the market with free copies; state theft hands a rival your model.
- **Lobbying**: builds political capital. 12 AI bills based on real 2025–2026 laws arrive as decisions, including SB 53, preemption, a datacenter moratorium, independent auditors and a kill-switch law.
- **2026 incidents**: rivals distilling your API, a model escaping its evaluation sandbox, and an insider spying on your executives.
