Your company now runs with you: alerts, an inbox, a monthly briefing and AI delegates. The race is fairer and harder, the leaderboard finally means something, the 2030s are full of things to do, rival labs are real businesses you can watch train, campuses reach 100 GW and your own AI keeps 500,000-GPU runs alive.

### Your company runs with you
- **Alerts that matter**: cash trouble, a stalled run, a rival taking #1 from you, contracts breaching their SLAs, a price war, an expiring term sheet or reservation, a board goal or bill vote coming up. Each shows as a banner with a link; you choose which ones pause the game. A problem that keeps coming back shows as a banner instead of stopping the clock again, so a long game stops about once a month, not every week.
- **An inbox instead of popups**: offers that can wait (rival rounds, partner deals, bills, poaching, moonshots…) no longer stop the clock. They wait three to four weeks and then resolve with the default they show you.
- **Standing answers**: tick "always answer this way" on a decision that keeps coming back. Manage them on the new Operations page.
- **Monthly briefing**: what changed last month and why: revenue by product with its causes (share, price, capacity, rival launches, price wars, your launches), what rivals did, what's coming, the risks, and what to do.
- **Delegates**: hand serving capacity, launches, cloud renewals, leasing idle chips, hiring, pay, enterprise leads and even planning your next training run to your COO. Its skill follows your best model: weekly scripts at first, a frontier agent acting every day later. Give the capacity delegate a cloud budget and it rents on-demand chips when serving pays for them, and returns them first when demand falls.
- **Contracts come first**: enterprise traffic is served before API and chat, like provisioned throughput, so a capacity crunch hits spot users before it breaks an SLA.
- **Chief of Staff, one click**: most of its warnings now come with a fix (deploy, launch, resume, lease, renew, raise, audit, switch products, turn on a delegate) and can be snoozed.
- **Rewind**: a rolling autosave every quarter, the last eight kept, so a bad call can be undone. ⌘S always saves.

### A fairer, harder race
- **Money is real**: your revenue can no longer exceed the AI market. Buyers have fixed budgets, chat and agent demand follow what people would pay, and auto-pricing keeps models 80–90% busy near the going rate, never below serving cost.
- **Compute is finite**: how much you can rent or buy follows world chip supply, not your valuation. One customer can rent about a quarter of a chip's cloud fleet and buy a share of each quarter's production; renting a big share pushes the price up, and clouds retire chips eight years after launch.
- **Rivals fight back**: they answer a lead you've held for a training cycle, copy what they can distill or download, and once you've been #1 for half a year the big labs bet runs on passing you. Rival Watch shows those bets coming.
- **Steadier valuations**: a private company's mark moves toward its new level over a few months, and term sheets price off it.
- **Fewer repeat interruptions**: talent raids at most every nine months, rival round invitations one at a time and half as often, and no mid-run data-mix popup.
- Many exploits closed: share offerings, warrant resales, brand farming with weak releases, unlimited RSI, cheap enterprise leads.

### A leaderboard that means something
- **Labs, not models**: one row per lab with its flagship, capability, index, gap to #1, price, speed and last release; expand a lab for its history, or switch to every model. "#N" means your place among labs everywhere in the game.
- **Capability**: a steady scale next to the index (GPT-4 = 100; +10 is ten times the effective compute) that never saturates.
- **A steadier index**: benchmarks rotate out one at a time when they saturate and newcomers phase in over weeks, so a model's score no longer jumps when the test set changes, and the index is never empty.
- **The race over time**: a chart of every lab's flagship capability, filters, and how far you are from #1 in compute terms.

### Rival Watch (new tab)
- **See rivals training**: every lab's next frontier model has a training run behind it: cluster size, pre-training compute, site, codename and progress.
- **Release radar**: a 12-month timeline of training phases and rumoured launch windows. The windows narrow as launches get closer.
- **Capability forecasts**: each upcoming model's expected Intelligence Index (with an 80% band), plotted against your best model and your next one.
- **Rumour mill**: leaks, analyst notes, red-team sightings and the occasional false rumour, which is exposed once the model ships.
- **Intel desk**: spend up to $50M a month, or let your own model read the signals, to get tighter dates, earlier word of new runs and leaked evals. A track record scores the rumour mill's calls.
- **Rivals react**: leapfrog a lab whose model is in post-training and it may declare a code red, either rushing its launch or holding it back to train harder. From October 2026 their launches can also slip.
- **Steal the show**: ship a better model in the two weeks before a rival's launch and you own the news cycle.

### Datacenters at gigawatt scale
- **Up to 100 GW per campus**: how big one site can get depends on its power. Local grids grow each year; on-site gas reaches 10 GW, small modular reactors (from 2030) 25 GW, and fusion (from 2034) 100 GW.
- **Phased construction**: big campuses come online a phase at a time, and each phase is paid for as it starts.
- **Add and remove**: expand in steps from 100 kW to 100 GW, cancel phases that haven't started, sell a campus, end a colocation lease, and move chips between sites.
- **Orders follow world supply**: each quarter's production of a chip is shared among buyers (more with supply deals and partners); the store has a one-click "most you can order" button.

### Training on 500,000 GPUs
- **The problem**: at 500K chips a fault hits every few minutes. Without help, a run spends all its time restarting.
- **Ops agents**: point a training-ops swarm at a run. Agents drain failing nodes before they crash, restart from in-memory snapshots in seconds, rewind loss spikes and quarantine nodes that silently corrupt gradients.
- **Coverage**: a run needs one engineer-equivalent per 600 chips, and smarter models cover more per agent. With a 2028-class model, about 1,300 agents take a 500K-GPU run from 0% to roughly 90% goodput.
- **Silent data corruption**: on runs of 20K+ chips, faults that don't crash anything quietly cost capability unless they're caught.
- Each run shows its ops coverage with an "Assign agents" button, and the Model Lab warns you when a run is too big to survive without agents.

### Agent swarms, smarter
- **No research gate**: any model can run as agents. Models without tool-use training work through a prompt-only scaffold at 60% output.
- **Intelligence matters**: each model gets an agent tier (Intern → Superhuman researcher) and a task horizon. Its strengths decide what it's best at: reasoning-heavy models make better researchers, coders make better chip designers.
- **Experience**: swarms get up to 25% better the longer they keep the same mission.
- **New missions**: security operations (attacks that get past your defences succeed less often) and competitive intelligence (sharper Rival Watch forecasts).
- **Full autonomy** opens with an engineer-level model, not only through research. Up to 12 swarms and a billion agents.
